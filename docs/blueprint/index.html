Pool Shed blueprint: one back office, built around the record

Recommendation: rebuild the screens, not the data. Every order, customer, project, product and supplier gets one page where its next step is one obvious button, as in Brightpearl. Then add what Brightpearl lacks for a pool company: project stages and budgets (simPRO), parts ordered against jobs and stock on vans (Joblogic), reorder points and landed costs (Cin7, Unleashed), and custom statuses that organise sales orders and purchase orders the way Brightpearl does, each tied to its next step. Engineers get just two things here: their van report and their van stock. Jobs and pool readings stay in the engineer app; Pool Shed is the office.

Eight areas replace seventeen sections

Each area opens on a short list of what needs doing, not a strip of tabs. Search across everything stays at the top. Settings and Azzy sit apart from the menu.

Custom statuses organise sales and purchase orders, and each one knows what happens next

As in Brightpearl, you name, colour and order your own statuses, separately for sales orders and purchase orders, and lists can be filtered and grouped by them. Pool Shed goes further: every status belongs to a stage on the order's status rail, can move the order on automatically, sets the one next button, and reminds the owner if an order sits in it too long. Today Pool Shed already has 14 sales statuses with colours and reminders and 9 purchase statuses, but purchase colours are fixed in the code and nothing happens automatically. Levels you can trust

On hand, allocated, free and on order for every product in every location (warehouse bins, receiving, quarantine, each van, each job). Free stock is what can be sold.

Every movement logged

Goods in, put away, transfer, pick, ship, van use, return, damage and count. Each has who, when, why and a reference. Stock is never just typed over.

Reorder thresholds

Per product and location: reorder at, order up to, preferred supplier, pack size and lead time. When free stock plus on order drops to the threshold, the item joins the reorder list.

One click to order

The reorder list groups by supplier and suggests quantities; one click makes the purchase orders. Van thresholds make warehouse top-up lists instead.

Counts and adjustments

Cycle counts by bin or category, full stock takes, van counts on the phone. Adjustments need a reason; large ones need approval, and each posts to the ledger.

Value and warnings

Average cost including freight; stock value by location; slow and dead stock; batch and expiry for chemicals; serials for pumps and heaters.

Fulfilment tells the customer at each step, with a tick box every time

With an online shop coming, pick, pack and ship need to run fast and keep the customer informed. Pool Shed already has goods notes, picking lists, pick, pack and ship screens, tracking fields and message templates for each step. What it lacks: those messages are only logged as "ready to send" and never actually emailed. The fix is to send them for real, always with a tick box so staff choose, and to pass status and tracking back to the shop.

Four processes run the business, and each starts and ends on a record

Sell

  1. Quote in Quote Studio, sent with the customer portal.
  2. Accepted: one click makes the order, or a project for builds. Nothing re-typed.
  3. Allocated from stock, or bought for this order (awaiting parts shows on the order).
  4. Picked, packed and shipped with a goods note and tracking, the customer emailed at each step if ticked; or fitted by an engineer.
  5. Invoiced from the order page; paid recorded once, matched to the invoice.

Build (projects)

  1. Accepted quote becomes the contract; stages and budget come from it.
  2. Deposit invoiced at once; each stage invoiced when reached.
  3. Costs arrive automatically: purchases, stock used from vans, labour days, hire.
  4. Extras quoted and approved add to the contract.
  5. Margin is live all the way, with a warning when it drifts below target.

Buy and pay

  1. Need: an order, a project stage, or stock below its reorder point (warehouse or van).
  2. PO raised and emailed; or record a purchase for things already bought.
  3. Goods in against the PO, with freight added to the cost.
  4. Bill matched to PO and goods in; differences flagged.
  5. Payment run: pro-forma paid up front, on-account on terms, approved by a second person.

Stock

  1. Locations: warehouse bins, receiving, quarantine, each van, each job.
  2. Goods in put away to a bin; transfer to a van by scan.
  3. Used from the van: shown by the van stock take (or a quick "used" entry), costed to the job when known.
  4. Van below minimum: top-up list for the warehouse, or a PO.
  5. Counts: weekly van checks and cycle counts; differences post to the ledger.
